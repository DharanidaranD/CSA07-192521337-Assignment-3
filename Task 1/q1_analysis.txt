Q1 - Which Socket Gets the Segment? (Work Out, Conclusion & Alternative Concept Analysis)
===========================================================================================

1. Work Out
-----------
a) Socket Routing (Demultiplexing):
- TCP from 49.37.2.8:50001 to port 443 -> Routed to socket: TCP 203.0.113.5:443 <-> 49.37.2.8:50001 ESTABLISHED.
- TCP from 117.96.4.2:50000 to port 443 -> Routed to socket: TCP 203.0.113.5:443 <-> 117.96.4.2:50000 ESTABLISHED.
- TCP SYN from 106.51.7.7:41000 to port 443 -> Routed to welcoming socket: TCP 203.0.113.5:443 LISTEN to begin a new 3-way handshake.
- UDP from 49.37.2.8:50000 to port 5004 -> Routed to single shared socket: UDP 203.0.113.5:5004.

b) TCP vs. UDP Demultiplexing:
- TCP sockets are identified by a 4-tuple: (Source IP, Source Port, Destination IP, Destination Port). Even when two clients both pick source port 50000, their Source IPs (49.37.2.8 vs. 117.96.4.2) differ, producing two distinct 4-tuples and two separate OS sockets.
- UDP sockets are identified by a 2-tuple: (Destination IP, Destination Port). All datagrams sent to 203.0.113.5:5004 enter the single UDP socket buffer; the application uses recvfrom() to inspect each sender's (IP, port) tuple.

c) Header Overhead Calculation (100-byte payload over IPv4):
- IPv4 Header = 20 bytes.
- UDP: 20 (IPv4) + 8 (UDP) = 28 bytes header. Total packet = 128 bytes.
  Overhead = (28 / 128) * 100 = 21.88%.
- TCP: 20 (IPv4) + 20 (TCP) = 40 bytes header. Total packet = 140 bytes.
  Overhead = (40 / 140) * 100 = 28.57%.

d) Protocol Choice per Feature:
- Live Scores -> TCP: Must arrive reliably without corrupted or skipped digits; low update frequency makes TCP overhead negligible.
- Ball-by-Ball Commentary -> TCP: Text events must appear in strict chronological order with zero missing balls.
- Live Video -> UDP: Real-time media cannot wait for retransmissions; stale retransmitted frames arrive past their playout deadline and stall playback.

2. Conclusion
-------------
Although UDP lacks built-in retransmissions, TCP achieves reliability by pausing delivery in the kernel receive buffer until lost segments are retransmitted (head-of-line blocking). For live video or voice calls, stalling playback for 60+ ms to recover one lost packet creates noticeable freezes, whereas UDP skips the lost frame and plays incoming frames immediately with lower header overhead (21.88% vs. 28.57%).

3. Alternative Concept Analysis (Live Video over TCP at 2% Loss, 60 ms RTT, 500 pkt/s)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Freeze duration per lost segment: At least 1 RTT = 60 ms (via fast retransmit after 3 duplicate ACKs), or 200 ms to 1,000 ms if a retransmission timeout (RTO) occurs.
- Freezes per minute:
  Total packets/min = 500 pkt/s * 60 s = 30,000 packets.
  Lost packets/min = 30,000 * 0.02 = 600 freezes per minute (10 freezes per second).
  Playback freezes continuously and becomes unwatchable.

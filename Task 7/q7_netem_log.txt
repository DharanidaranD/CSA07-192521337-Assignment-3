========================================================================
Q7 – Choose the Protocols from First Principles (Design & Analysis)
========================================================================

Stage 1 – Interpret the Problem (2 Marks)
-----------------------------------------
Traffic Requirements Matrix:
1. Live Video Consultation:
   - Delay Sensitivity: Ultra-low (< 150 ms one-way latency, < 30 ms jitter)
   - Loss Tolerance: Tolerates up to 5% packet loss with FEC/PLC
   - Ordering: Playout timestamp order only (no retransmission stalls)
   - Security: End-to-end encrypted (SRTP / DTLS)

2. X-Ray Images & Electronic Health Records (EHR):
   - Delay Sensitivity: Moderate (< 15 s for a 10-15 MB DICOM study)
   - Loss Tolerance: Zero loss (100% bit-exact integrity via SHA-256)
   - Ordering: Strict byte-stream ordering
   - Security: Encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3, HIPAA/DISHA compliant)

3. Doctor-Nurse Clinical Chat:
   - Delay Sensitivity: Low (< 500 ms delivery)
   - Loss Tolerance: Zero loss
   - Ordering: Strict message sequence order
   - Security: Encrypted in transit (TLS 1.3)

4. Service Lookup (DNS):
   - Delay Sensitivity: Very low (< 100 ms cached lookup)
   - Loss Tolerance: Retries on timeout
   - Ordering: Single request/response transaction
   - Security: DNSSEC / DNS-over-HTTPS (DoH)

Stage 2 – Compare Candidate Protocols (3 Marks)
-----------------------------------------------
* Protocol Architecture Mapping:
  - Live Video: UDP + RTP/SRTP (WebRTC). Eliminates TCP handshake overhead
    and head-of-line (HoL) blocking under 5% 4G packet loss.
  - X-Ray & Patient Records: HTTP/3 over QUIC (with TLS 1.3). Multiplexes
    concurrent image uploads without cross-stream HoL blocking and uses
    modern congestion control (BBR) to sustain throughput on lossy 4G links.
  - Clinical Chat: QUIC streams / WebSockets over TLS 1.3. Guarantees
    reliable, ordered message delivery with 0-RTT/1-RTT reconnection when
    4G signal fluctuates.

* Unscaled TCP Throughput Limit (W = 64 KB, RTT = 300 ms):
  - Window size W = 64 KB = 65,536 bytes = 524,288 bits.
  - Max Throughput = W / RTT = 524,288 bits / 0.300 s = 1,747,627 bps
    ~= 1.75 Mbps.
  - Impact on X-Ray Transfers: A 15 MB (120 Mb) chest X-ray requires at
    least 120 Mb / 1.75 Mbps ~= 68.6 seconds over an unscaled 64 KB TCP
    window, plus severe throughput collapse at 5% loss. Enabling TCP Window
    Scaling (RFC 7323) or using QUIC is mandatory.

* Clinic DNS & DiffServ QoS Configuration:
  - DNS: Deploy a local caching DNS forwarder at each rural clinic
    (TTL = 300 s) with DoH upstream fallback to survive transient WAN drops.
  - QoS: Configure DiffServ EF (strict priority) for live video/audio,
    AF41 for clinical chat, and AF21 (token-bucket shaped) for background
    X-ray uploads so bulk transfers never starve live consultations.

Stage 4 – Defend the Design & Alternative Concept Analysis (2 Marks)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
* Design Justification:
  - Separating real-time media (UDP/SRTP) from bulk medical files
    (HTTP/3 over QUIC) prevents X-ray uploads from freezing live video
    while overcoming the 1.75 Mbps unscaled TCP window bottleneck.

* Impact of Upgrading Rural Clinics from 4G to 5G:
  - RTT drops from 100-300 ms down to 10-20 ms, and packet loss falls
    below 0.1%.
  - High-bitrate 4K diagnostic video becomes practical, 15 MB X-rays finish
    transferring in under 1 second, and Forward Error Correction (FEC)
    redundancy overhead can be reduced to save bandwidth.

* Alternative Concept Analysis (Commercial Telehealth Vendor Service):
  - Three Critical Technical Questions Before Signing:
    1. Does the platform support adaptive bitrate (ABR) and WebRTC FEC
       tuned for 300 ms RTT and 5% packet loss on rural 4G links?
    2. Are video, chat, and DICOM files end-to-end encrypted (E2EE) with
       zero third-party data retention to satisfy healthcare privacy laws?
    3. Are media relay (TURN) and edge servers hosted regionally in India
       to avoid international routing latency?
  - Decision Criteria: Adopt the commercial vendor only if SLA benchmarks
    demonstrate < 150 ms glass-to-glass latency under 5% simulated loss and
    a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) guarantees patient privacy.
